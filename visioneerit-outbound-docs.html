01 · Overview

One engine. Every VisioneerIT product.

A reusable, config-driven outbound engine — source, verify, personalize, send, read the reply, book the meeting — built from seven tools that each do the one job they're best at, connected by n8n. OryonIQ is the current pilot; swap the config file and the same engine runs any VisioneerIT product.

Nothing in this system is a single opaque black box. Every step is either something the agent can freely do because it's free and reversible, or something a human explicitly approves because it spends money or reaches a real person. That split — not any one clever tool — is the actual architecture.

02 · How It Runs

The agent acts alone until it would cost money or reach someone.

Sourcing, verifying, drafting, and reading a reply are all free and reversible — the agent just does them and reports. The moment an action would spend a credit or land in front of a real prospect, it stops at a gate and waits for an explicit human approve, adjust, or deny.

What the agent decides alone vs. what waits for a human

DecisionWho
Which product config and market signal to run this cycleagent — reports it
A free Apollo search + pre-filter on the free flagsagent
Drafting personalized openersagent
Building a plan — who, how many, estimated credit costagent proposes
Spending Apollo credits (revealing an email or phone)human approves
Sending email or enrolling into Instantlyhuman approves
Any outbound phone callnot in the system
Anything that reaches a real prospect or spends moneyhuman approves

The rule, in one line: free and reversible → the agent acts and reports; costly or outward-facing → the agent proposes and waits. Full design in VIO-operator-agent.md, including the exact system prompt.

03 · One Lead, Start To Finish

Follow one lead through the whole pipeline.

OryonIQ's live config today: Capture Managers, VPs and Directors of Business Development, and Proposal Managers, at US government contractors. The signal is dated and real, not a template.

  1. Source — free
    Apollo's free people search, filtered to the config's ICP: Capture Manager, VP/Director of Business Development, Proposal Manager — US, keyword "government contracting." Returns names, titles, and whether an email or direct-dial phone is even reachable. Zero credits spent.
  2. Filter
    Kept only if the free has_email flag is true. Everyone else is dropped before anything costs a cent.
  3. Personalize
    OpenAI writes one specific opening line per lead, grounded in the config's live signal — not a merge field.
    signal fed to the model
    "The Pentagon suspended CMMC Phase II on July 13, 2026, and DoD committed $32B to AI, cloud, and cyber in H1 FY2026 — federal set-asides are opening fast."
    drafted opener — Marcus Reyes, Capture Manager
    "Marcus — with CMMC Phase II paused and $32B moving into AI and cyber, the set-asides your capture team tracks are shifting faster than the usual RFP cadence."
  4. Assemble
    The opener plus the config's offer and call-to-action, signed off by the sending identity — same three fields for every lead in this campaign, only the opener changes per person.
    offer · cta · sender (from config-oryoniq.json)
    "OryonIQ flags the pursuits that fit your NAICS and SDB status before the RFP drops, so you're bidding while everyone else is still searching." → visioneerit.com/contact — Ellen, OryonIQ, VisioneerIT
  5. Gate — human approves
    The agent posts the batch, not the send: who it touches, the projected cost, why now. A human replies approve, adjust, or deny before anything leaves the building.
  6. Send
    Instantly sends and warms the email leg; Sendr runs the LinkedIn touch and can generate a personalized page from the prospect's own site. Both fire from the same approved batch.
  7. Reply arrives
    Instantly's webhook catches it; OpenAI reads the reply and classifies sentiment, out-of-office status, and whether a phone number is on file.
  8. Gate — human approves
    Only a positive, non-out-of-office reply from a real lead reaches this gate at all. It lands in Slack as Approve / Decline, and a person decides.
  9. Human takes it from here
    The alert goes to a person, who replies personally. There is no automated voice anywhere in the system — that leg was removed in August 2026, so the compliance question stops being "is this call gated correctly?" and becomes "there is no call."
  10. Booked
    Logged to the Sheet with everything upstream of it, so cost-per-meeting is a real computed number, not a guess.

04 · Why This Shape

Seven narrow tools, not one wide one.

Each tool here does exactly the job it's built best at, and nothing else: Apollo finds people, Reoon confirms they're real, OpenAI writes and reads language, Instantly and Sendr carry the message on their channel, and a person takes over the moment someone replies. None of them is asked to be the whole system — n8n is what connects them, and increasingly, an agent that reasons about the connection.

That has a direct consequence: nothing here is opaque. A single all-in-one platform that sources, writes, sends, and handles replies autonomously is fast to stand up, but there's no seam to insert a human, and no way to audit a draft before it ships. Composing narrow tools costs more wiring — it's why reach-engine's config validates before anything runs, and why the two gates in section 02 exist at all — but it means every step is inspectable, and the two moments that actually carry risk (spending money, reaching a person) are exactly where a human sits.

The config is the contract

The engine doesn't know it's selling OryonIQ. Everything product-specific — the ICP, the signal, the offer, the pitch — lives in one config file that the engine validates before it will run at all. Swap config-oryoniq.json for a different product's config and the same engine, the same gates, and the same tools run a different campaign. No code change.

06 · Personalization

A real signal in, a reviewed draft out.

Every opener is grounded in a specific, dated market event — never a merge field wearing a first name. The model is told exactly what to avoid: no fluff, no em-dashes, no "leverage / unlock / streamline," and never invent a fact about the company it doesn't actually know. If the signal is stale, the openers read generic — so keeping the signal current is the highest-leverage maintenance this system has.

And because nothing sends without the human gate in section 02, personalization quality is reviewable before it ever reaches a prospect, not audited after the fact.

07 · Compliance

The rules that don't bend for convenience.

  • No automated voice, at all. The system places no calls. The voice leg was removed in August 2026, so TCPA exposure is structurally zero rather than merely gated — there is no dial path to misfire. A positive reply routes to a person, who follows up themselves.
  • Suppression is append-only and matches on any identifier. A lead suppressed by email must not resurface under their phone, LinkedIn, or domain.
  • Every inbound webhook authenticates first, fail-closed. If the expected secret isn't even configured, the workflow refuses rather than defaulting open — a forged event must never be able to trigger a real send or a real call.
  • Secrets are read, never copied. Workflows read a credential to make a call; a key never gets pasted into a doc, a chat, or a published page.

08 · Targets & Results

Two numbers, reported separately — never one "100%."

A literal 100% reply or meeting rate isn't a real target for cold outreach. What can genuinely run near 100% is quality control; what outreach itself can do is beat the market's average by several times over. Reporting them as one blended number hides which one moved.

Bucket A — Gates
target: as close to 100% as real
  • Inbox placement 90%+
  • Bounce rate under 2%
  • Every message has a real signal-anchored hook
  • Compliance fully defensible, every send
  • No triggered lead silently dropped
Bucket B — Outcomes
target: top decile, not 100%
  • Reply rate 8–12% (market average ≈ 3.4%)
  • Meeting rate 2–4% of sends
  • At <500/mo sniper volume: roughly 10–15 meetings/mo
No pilot has run yet. This section reports the real batch's numbers once the ~25-lead pilot executes — not a projection ahead of it.
